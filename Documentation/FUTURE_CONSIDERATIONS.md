# Future considerations

## Rendering

- **Make the branch structure readable at 24–41 m on a separately authored oak.** At 0:30–0:31 in Toni Macaroni’s showcase, the pale sky shows through irregular openings around the central tree’s forks. The eye can follow a limb from trunk to tip and see the tufts it supports. Try moving or thinning selected sprays around two or three major forks, varying bough reach and tuft size, and retaining dense pockets elsewhere. A more extensive rebuild of the secondary branches is worth testing if moving sprays leaves the crown uniformly filled. Compare a duplicate against the current forked white oak with identical light, exposure, GPU cut policy and several viewing angles. Judge visible branch continuity, gaps at different scales and the outer silhouette before changing overall leaf density. [Showcase at 0:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VC_NUxbeA0E&t=30s)

- **Carry the measured crown cut into a multi-tree GPU submission test.** The 41/100 m triangle-only experiment qualified a reusable canopy with native-selected trunk geometry at 13 angles per distance. It reached 2.660M / 1.098M triangles against native 2.514M / 1.073M. Move the resident hierarchy traversal and assembly index gathering off the CPU, measure moving-camera frame time with multiple trees, and compare the 24 m pose and the voxel-enabled asset as separate controls. Keep the interior bough's isolated quality gate when reusing a canopy recipe. [Live triangle comparison](OAK_LIVE_TRIANGLE_SELECTION.md)

- **Reduce far-bough brick work at matched silhouette quality.** The 100 m exposed-bough experiment exchanged 365 of 3,299 leaf bricks. Primary-camera IoU improved from 0.781 to 0.803, but the cut missed the triangle silhouette target at all 13 views and took longer to draw. Fit first-visible depth and material, keep the exposed rims and tips as triangles, and compare the same accepted brick cut across the box and batched visibility renderers. [Whole-grove transition, cost and lighting](OAK_VOXEL_TRANSITION_AND_LIGHTING.md) · [Exposed bough controls](OAK_BOUGH_VOXEL_RESULTS.md)

- **Close the forked white oak's 24–41 m triangle-efficiency gap.** At matched whole-crown silhouette on the 4K quarter view, our two-bough pilot submits 8.021M triangles versus Nanite's 6.527M at 24 m (+22.9%), and 2.702M versus 2.139M at 41 m (+26.3%). The voxel-enabled optimized Nanite tree submitted zero bricks at both poses. Extend the reusable-cut test to more boughs, starting with leaf cost and the size of the groups that choose detail together. Recheck the full crown, held-out views and shaded camera time at matched occupied-pixel overlap and one-pixel outline error. Track this independently of the 100 m voxel experiment. [Matched triangle measurements](OAK_REUSABLE_SPRAYS_41_100.md#follow-up-on-triangle-coverage-before-voxelization) · [Voxel experiment plan](OAK_BOUGH_VOXEL_EXPERIMENT.md)

- **Modeled foliage shimmer and temporal anti-aliasing (TAA).** Modeled Oak shimmers more than Card Oak during camera motion. Subpixel leaf edges and canopy gaps can flicker even with 4× MSAA. Compare a slow orbit with wind paused and MSAA off and on, then toggle foliage shadow reception to separate coverage flicker from shadow sampling inside the canopy. Consider TAA with subpixel camera jitter and motion-vector reprojection. Test with wind paused first, then account for wind deformation and check for ghosting or blur as gaps open between leaves. For distant foliage, evaluate LODs that preserve apparent canopy coverage and larger gaps.

- **Normal offset bias for shadows.** Evaluate offsetting the receiver's shadow lookup position along its surface normal, with an offset related to the light angle and shadow-map texel size. This may reduce self-shadowing artifacts while retaining contact shadows. Compare it across sun angles on the cube, bark, and both sides of foliage cards, and watch for detached or distorted shadows.

- **Cluster LOD culling.** Modeled Oak Walk has wind-aware camera frustum culling for fixed foliage groups. Use the flat CPU test as the reference for light-frustum group culling or a BVH when a larger workload warrants it. Test camera and light views separately so off-camera groups can still cast visible shadows. Spatial bounds can reject whole subtrees for visibility, and conservative projected parent-error bounds can skip descendants whose coarser representations already meet the LOD target. Evaluate hierarchical depth occlusion separately. Compare selected, visible, and submitted counts and verify that drawing with culling gives the same visible result as the complete cut. [Oak walk results](../README.md#modeled-oak-walk) · [Epic’s speaker notes, *Hierarchical culling* and *Two-Pass Occlusion Culling*](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)

- **Draw batching and shadow-specific foliage LOD.** Revisit these optimizations when a full-crown or stand benchmark shows a measurable draw-list, submission, or shadow-pass bottleneck. Use indexed draw records, API calls, CPU draw-list time, and GPU pass time to decide which change to test. Repeat the [Oak Branch shadow calibration](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#shadow-lod-calibration) at that scale and when changing the shadow projection or resolution. Check low-sun receiver coverage, wind stability, and the moment a caster crosses the camera frustum before enabling an automatic policy. Use sampled shadow-texel error alongside the image and timing comparisons.

## Tree asset pipeline

- **Reuse the baker and renderer across trees.** `BakeFixedLod` simplifies a named mesh, supports UV and rim constraints, and records sampled source and transition errors. The renderer builds spatial groups from spray placements and selects levels from projected error in the camera and light views. Keep the 1 px camera budget as a starting point and validate it with matched renders. Switch distances follow from geometry error, placement scale, and camera projection. The forked oak’s 1,504-triangle leaf LOD 4 and 950- and 422-triangle wood LODs were tuned once for its shared spray. Try those recipes on a comparable broadleaf spray and select triangle targets from its own error and coverage measurements. The fitted leaf proxies require an open leaf mesh with a compatible UV layout. Needle clusters and other leaf layouts may need different recipes. [Baker](../Tools/BakeFixedLod.cpp) · [Forked oak recipes](../CMakeLists.txt)

- **Describe trees and sprays in asset data.** The viewer currently registers Forked White Oak explicitly and loads one leaf primitive and one wood primitive with a shared placement table. Its bough prototype uses one membership list tied to that export. Store each tree’s spray references, placement transforms, and optional bough membership in asset data, and allow a tree to reference several spray designs. Sidecars currently fingerprint the tree GLB. To reuse an identical spray across tree exports, key its bake by source geometry and recipe and store tree placements separately. [Scene loading](../src/ViewerScene.cpp) · [Bough bake](../Tools/BakeSprayBough.cpp)

- **Validate the split on a second tree.** Use a tree with more than one spray design. Start with the shared broadleaf recipe where the inputs fit, then measure which limits need to vary by spray. Verify that each spray bakes once and that camera and shadow culling, LOD transitions, and coverage hold up across placements. Use those results to decide which settings belong to the spray recipe and which belong to the tree asset.
