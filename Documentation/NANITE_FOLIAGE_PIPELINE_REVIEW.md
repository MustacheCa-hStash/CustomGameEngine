# Nanite Foliage source audit and Oak Branch pipeline review

Reviewed Epic’s Unreal Engine `5.8.4-release`, commit `f45729c0b`, from the local checkout. The source trace covers build-time selection and voxelization, encoding, GPU rasterization, material shading, and Virtual Shadow Maps. The Oak Branch comparison uses the current working tree and saved benchmark results. The brick bounds and region counts below were computed from the local bake files. The later sections record renderer experiments performed after the source audit.

## How Nanite chooses voxels

**The representation decision happens per reduction group during the build.** `FClusterDAG::ReduceGroup` tries simplifying triangle children to the parent triangle budget. Its triangle cost is a distance-like value from the mesh simplifier. The voxel search starts from an estimate based on group surface area and vertex count, floored by the largest inherited LOD error. It increases cell edge length until occupied cells and bricks fit their budgets, with up to 128 bricks per regular voxel cluster. In the normal comparison path, voxels win only when that cell edge is smaller than the triangle simplification error. Voxelization can be forced by build settings, and higher groups with voxel children continue reducing into voxels. All parents produced by the same reduction share a bound and an error, with the error at least as large as the inherited one. [Builder settings and reduction](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/NaniteBuilder.cpp#L627) · [Group reduction](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/ClusterDAG.cpp#L1378) · [Triangle simplifier](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Cluster.cpp#L911)

At runtime, GPU hierarchy traversal evaluates the projected bounds and error of the already-built clusters. The same selection machinery handles triangles and voxels. There is no separate foliage distance selector in this path. The cell edge gives the builder a practical proxy for voxel LOD error. Our held-out views already show gaps and net density can vary substantially by angle at the same cell size, so keep image-space coverage and gap checks as independent acceptance criteria for Oak Branch. [Projected LOD tests](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteClusterCulling.usf#L258) · [Oak Branch coverage results](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#coverage-optimized-binary-bake)

## How the bake preserves coverage and materials

**The default voxel bake turns fractional coverage into solid-cell occupancy.** The builder seeds candidate cells from child triangles and voxel bounds. For the ray-traced path it samples the build’s Embree triangle scene, which includes leaf triangles and transformed assembly geometry. The default mesh settings use 64 rays, with separable sampling and voxel opacity both off. The general sampler uses low-discrepancy rays with varying directions clipped to each cell. A separate optional path uses axis-aligned probes and can force full coverage for certain continuous surfaces. [Mesh defaults](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Runtime/Engine/Classes/Engine/EngineTypes.h#L3368) · [Ray generation](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Cluster.cpp#L1483) · [Embree scene](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/NaniteRayTracingScene.cpp#L21)

With opacity disabled, Epic starts with cells that received hits, sums their coverage, then removes the least-covered cells until the retained count fits that sum. As it removes a cell, it distributes coverage and normal statistics to eligible neighbors based on the projected area of the removed cell’s normal distribution. Removed cells can still seed the next coarser level. This differs from our fixed cutoff and from fitting a binary mask against specific camera poses. It gives us an offline candidate to compare using the same 0.125-unit grid, held-out views, and brick budget, without changing the runtime format. Epic also has an optional bake setting to store coverage in vertex color. The default raster path audited here uses binary occupancy. [Coverage filtering and redistribution](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Cluster.cpp#L1929)

The final cells carry sampled triangle material and interpolated vertex attributes. Bricks are grouped by material, use a 64-bit occupancy mask, and pack occupied-cell attributes densely. Epic shifts the brick origin to its occupied minimum and encodes the tight extent. Shading uses the visibility-buffer brick ID and depth to recover the cell, then counts preceding set bits to find its attributes. The default shading path samples a view-conditioned SGGX normal distribution fitted from the probe hits, with noise varying by pixel and frame. The analytic variant is compiled off in this release. Our deterministic two-normal mixture avoids adding a time-varying shading signal. A normal-model experiment would need motion and temporal stability checks alongside still-frame quality. [Brick construction](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Cluster.cpp#L2109) · [Brick encoding](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Encode/NaniteEncode.cpp#L101) · [Cell reconstruction](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteVertexDeformation.ush#L507) · [Normal sampling](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteVertexFactory.ush#L898) · [Analytic shading setting](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Shared/NaniteDefinitions.h#L14)

## How the GPU renders the bricks

**Nanite schedules compute work over tight brick rectangles.** The culling pass tags surviving clusters with depth buckets. Raster binning separates voxel work and arranges it by bucket when depth bucketing is enabled. The voxel compute rasterizer projects each brick to a conservative rectangle, rejects pixels against the current depth using the brick’s nearest possible depth, and compacts surviving work into wave-sized batches. It then traverses the brick mask with a bounded grid walk and atomically writes the winner into the visibility buffer. Our `Voxel.hlsl` draws the far faces of one full box per brick and runs the grid walk in a pixel shader. Sorting those draws can be measured independently, but Nanite’s pre-traversal depth read and work compaction require a separate implementation in our renderer. [Depth bucketing](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteClusterCulling.usf#L853) · [Raster bin allocation](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteRasterBinning.usf#L461) · [Brick rectangles and work queue](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteRasterizer.usf#L1592) · [Visibility and depth writes](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteWritePixel.ush)

**Nanite writes depth at the midpoint of the ray segment through the first occupied cell.** Our GPU shader and CPU reference use cell entry, and our current shaded path uses that point for shadow receiving by default. The midpoint changes the position through the cell according to the ray direction. It is worth testing as a receiver-only change before trying it for camera depth. Moving camera depth can change which surface wins where a voxel interleaves with bark or leaves. [Nanite voxel hit processing](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteRasterizer.usf#L1193) · [Oak Branch voxel shader](../shaders/Voxel.hlsl#L205) · [CPU reference trace](../Tools/FoliageTrace.cpp#L141)

**Nanite can rasterize voxels into Virtual Shadow Maps.** It uses the same brick path specialized for depth-only shadow output, with light-view culling, virtual page routing, and a separate shadow LOD scale. Our voxel comparison keeps foliage shadow casters on triangles, which isolates camera changes but leaves a different geometry proxy on either side of the shadow lookup. Nanite also encodes a representative bone per brick for skinned foliage. A crown or wind experiment can stay separate from the current rest-pose comparison. [Virtual Shadow Map raster pass](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Runtime/Renderer/Private/VirtualShadowMaps/VirtualShadowMapArray.cpp#L4239) · [Shadow LOD scale](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Runtime/Renderer/Private/Shadows/ShadowSceneRenderer.cpp#L142) · [Per-brick bone encoding](https://github.com/EpicGames/UnrealEngine/blob/5.8.4-release/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Encode/NaniteEncodeSkinning.cpp#L238)

## What the Oak Branch assets and benchmarks show

- **Tight brick bounds are the clearest performance experiment.** The current fitted 0.125-unit bake has 365 bricks and 1,888 occupied cells. I decoded each mask and summed the projected area of its tight cell AABB along each grid axis. The result was 40.8%, 45.4%, and 41.5% of the full-box area on X, Y, and Z respectively. These are axis-aligned area estimates before perspective, overlap, and occlusion. The submission would still contain the same number of proxy triangles. Earlier runs show why this matters: at 120 units with 25 branches and 4× MSAA, the shaded camera pass took 1.089 ms with fine voxel proxies, 0.339 ms with coarse proxies, and 0.111 ms with LOD 6. [Existing GPU results](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#gpu-voxel-comparison-phase-iii)
- **A regional prototype is already in the working tree.** It divides the ordered fixed groups into four ranges and assigns each occupied cell of the fitted whole-branch bake to whichever region sampled the most coverage in that cell. The regional cut switches a range when its member groups can use LOD 6 and the region passes the projected cell-size gate, with 5% exit hysteresis. I counted 39 source bricks with multiple region owners. Drawing the four split lists would use 405 brick records versus 365 in the whole bake. The renderer uses the whole bake when all regions have switched. A dominant owner gives each cell a unique region, but a mixed cut can still drop or duplicate projected coverage at shared boundaries because cells can represent leaves from multiple regions. [Region assignment](../Tools/VoxelBake.cpp#L495) · [Regional selection](../src/D3D12Renderer.cpp#L586) · [Camera draw lists](../src/D3D12Renderer.cpp#L1583)
- **The saved timing advantage applies to the whole-branch comparison.** In `build/phase1/oak-branch-transition-a.csv`, the 27-sample pooled far medians were 0.0842 ms automatic versus 0.1096 ms triangles for the camera, and 0.1184 ms versus 0.1440 ms for the GPU frame. A second saved run showed the same direction. The regional CSV I found has a header and zero data rows, so the mixed-region timing and quality still need measurement. The benchmark already checks mixed cuts and can log camera triangles, brick submissions, and frame time. [Transition benchmark](../src/BranchVoxelBenchmark.cpp#L788)
- **Shadow visibility is the larger measured shading discrepancy.** Across 28 captures, substituting the LOD 6 shadow visibility lowered mean absolute shaded luminance error from 0.01836 to 0.00791. The baked per-cell receiver improved only 16 of 28 captures and worsened the 700-unit retreat brightness deficit. View-dependent normal weights changed average shaded error very little. These measurements make the receiver-position and caster-representation experiments more relevant than expanding the normal payload first. [Directional normal weights](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#view-dependent-voxel-normal-weights) · [Shadow receiver comparison](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#baked-voxel-shadow-receivers)

## Tight brick proxy results

The renderer now packs occupied bounds into four extra bytes per brick at upload. The vertex shader pads the bounds by up to 1/64 cell inside the original brick and uses them for the proxy box. The voxel grid, asset format, traversal mask, and attribute indices are shared with the full-box control. [Upload](../src/D3D12Renderer.cpp#L1089) · [Proxy vertices](../shaders/Voxel.hlsl#L63)

Two RTX 5090 runs compared the fitted 0.125-unit bake at 3200 × 1800. These are pooled medians for 25 branches in the shaded 4× MSAA view, with the same triangle shadow casters and brick submissions in both paths.

| Distance | Camera full box | Camera tight box | GPU frame full box | GPU frame tight box |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 700 | 0.0986 ms | 0.0957 ms | 0.1392 ms | 0.1341 ms |
| 1,200 | 0.0934 ms | 0.0914 ms | 0.1323 ms | 0.1292 ms |
| 3,000 | 0.0780 ms | 0.0771 ms | 0.1167 ms | 0.1157 ms |

Resolved coverage agreed within the benchmark tolerance at 1× and 4× MSAA for the retreat, side, inside-brick, and far-clip poses. The largest mismatch was 25 of 61,318 covered pixels at 4×. The 1× depth capture recorded two outliers beyond 0.001 units at the retreat and one at the far clip. The worst difference was 0.518 units at the retreat, where 99% of shared hits agreed within 0.00015 units. The existing CPU/GPU reference check passed for the denser 0.015625- and 0.03125-unit bakes. Timing and parity files are under `build/phase1/oak-branch-tight-proxies-*.csv`. Reproduce the paired comparison with `--branch-voxel-bounds-benchmark report.csv`. ## Midpoint shadow sampling results

The **Shadow sample position** control now offers **Cell entry**, **Cell midpoint**, and **Baked leaf** where the bake has receiver data. Midpoint samples halfway through the camera ray’s clipped segment inside the first occupied voxel. The camera still writes entry depth. Reproduce the comparison with `--branch-lighting-diagnostics report.csv --midpoint`.

Two runs produced byte-identical reports over seven orbit poses at 250 and 700 units, with two sun azimuths. Coverage, encoded camera depth, and unshadowed renders were pixel-identical between modes, and the triangle shadow caster count stayed fixed. Over the 28 captures, mean absolute shaded luminance error against LOD 6 went from 0.01835 at entry to 0.01831 at midpoint. Midpoint improved 15 captures and worsened 13. Mean absolute shadow visibility error increased from 0.32819 to 0.33061. Average brightness deficit grew from 2.8% to 6.2%. At the 700-unit retreat with the default sun it grew from 9.6% to 16.7%. Cell entry remains the default. The paired reports are `build/phase1/oak-branch-shadow-midpoint-a.csv` and `build/phase1/oak-branch-shadow-midpoint-b.csv`. [Lighting diagnostic](../src/BranchVoxelBenchmark.cpp#L525)

## Triangle and voxel shadow caster comparison

The diagnostic replaced only foliage casters with the same fitted 0.125-unit voxel bake used in the camera. The depth-only shader casts parallel sun rays and writes the midpoint of the first occupied cell to the existing 2048 × 2048 orthographic shadow map. Bark and the receiver bias stayed fixed. Triangle casters retained their production raster depth bias. The voxel pass writes explicit depth with no extra caster bias. The test uses the current binary mask and shadow map, so the results characterize this implementation and bias setup.

Two runs reproduced all 28 captures byte for byte and matched the earlier triangle controls. Camera coverage and encoded voxel hit depth were identical across caster modes. A control with foliage casting disabled confirmed that voxel bricks contributed to received shadows. The table reports per-pose mean errors averaged over the route, using LOD 6 triangles as the reference on pixels covered by both camera representations.

| Foliage casters | Voxel receiver | Shadow visibility error | Shaded luminance error | Mean brightness difference |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| LOD 6 triangles | Cell entry | 0.32819 | 0.01835 | −2.8% |
| LOD 6 triangles | Cell midpoint | 0.33061 | 0.01831 | −6.2% |
| Voxels | Cell entry | 0.36659 | 0.01966 | −1.9% |
| Voxels | Cell midpoint | 0.38478 | 0.01990 | −8.4% |

Changing the caster representation did not make midpoint receiving better on this route. A camera ray and a sun ray can sample different points inside the same voxel, so matching occupancy alone leaves depth disagreements. Keep triangle casters and cell entry as the viewer defaults. Reproduce the diagnostic with `--branch-lighting-diagnostics report.csv --caster-matrix`. Paired CSVs are under `build/phase1/oak-branch-shadow-caster-matrix-*.csv`. ## Further experiments

The completed regional route and region 3 bake comparison are recorded in the [Oak Branch experiment plan](OAK_BRANCH_CLUSTER_LOD_PLAN.md#region-3-bake-ab).

1. **Validate the four-region prototype before changing the cut.** Capture a route with actual partial masks and collect timings against whole-branch automatic and forced LOD 6. Force all 16 masks at representative transition poses to check occupancy, boundary coverage, depth, and temporal changes. Record normalized pixel gain and loss, gaps, and duplication of brick records. Compare the current dominant-owner mask with one independently baked candidate for a pilot region, built from the same source leaf IDs as its triangle replacement. Compare both in isolation and against the full branch with neighbors present. Try local coverage redistribution as a bake variant at the same cell size and brick cap. This isolates the filtering change from candidate construction and provides a held-out comparison against the current per-view optimizer.
2. **Extract a CPU selector once candidate quality is measured.** Give each complete replacement bounds, projected geometric error, coverage calibration, and draw ranges. Reuse the current fixed-group and regional selection as parity controls. Permit any complete candidate that passes the projected limit and the calibrated coverage gate. Use measured batch costs to decide whether eligible voxel replacements are worthwhile. Keep bark on triangles and select camera and shadow cuts independently when introducing voxel shadow casting. A larger DAG or GPU culling effort should follow evidence that fixed regions or CPU selection limit the crown or grove workload.
