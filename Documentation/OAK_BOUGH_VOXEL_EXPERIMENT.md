# Exposed white oak bough voxel experiment

The exposed-bough experiment is complete. The triangle cut remains the recommended choice for bough 7. [Measured results and review command](OAK_BOUGH_VOXEL_RESULTS.md)

Test whether a voxel or mixed triangle-and-voxel cut of bough 7 at 100 m can beat our best reusable triangle cut in the shaded camera pass while retaining its silhouette quality, both in isolation and in the crown. The fixture is the optimized forked oak's 371 placements, two-sided leaves and one-sided twigs. Our isolated triangle control at the 132° camera draws 74,047 triangles in **0.0791 ms**. Replaying Nanite's 58,823 selected *triangles* in the same D3D camera took **0.0750 ms**. A native UE voxel-enabled bough profile is a separate measurement to collect. [Triangle controls](OAK_REUSABLE_SPRAYS_41_100.md#gpu-measurements)

## 1. Measure the Nanite voxel cut

Build a sibling of the existing UE bough assembly with **Voxelize on both the spray part and the bough**, preserving the source geometry, transforms, materials and original Preserve Area control. Capture finest reference masks, the native 1 px cut and warmed counters at 41, 50, 52, 56, 58 and 100 m. The existing **whole-tree** sweep makes 41 m the near control, 50–58 m the mixed-representation interval, and 100 m the far test (88.56% of visible tree pixels were voxels). Measure the isolated bough's own transition. [Whole-tree sweep](../build/unreal-nanite-oak/triangle-coverage-check/representation.csv) · [Assembly fixture](../Tools/MakeOakJointControl.py)

Extend the triangle readback to cover the voxel raster bins. For each selected voxel cluster record cell edge, occupied cells, bricks, material and assembly transform. Epic's voxel rasterizer uses the cluster's local `LODError` as the cell edge. Apply the actual transform axes, including instance scale, convert UE centimetres to metres, then project at the cluster's depth. Report local and world sizes and projected pixel footprints, with p10/median/p90 weighted by occupied cells. Count submitted instances separately from unique part data. Check the decoded brick and triangle totals against the offscreen counters and the visible voxel share against UE's visualization. This reveals whether the first custom bake should reuse a part or merge several placements. ## 2. Choose cell sizes and bake candidates

First replay the exact native mixed cut through D3D using the decoded brick masks, transforms, material slots and remaining triangles. Check the silhouette and first-hit depth against UE before interpreting timings. This gives the custom baker a same-renderer voxel control and shows how much of a performance difference follows the brick geometry itself.

In UE 5.8.4's default Voxelize path, each reduction group also gets a simplified triangle candidate. With group surface area A, the target occupied-cell count is approximately ¾ of the summed child vertex counts (minimum one). The first cell edge is the larger of the inherited LOD error and 0.75 × √(A / target cells). The builder tries 10% larger edges until the cells and bricks fit, while the edge remains below the competing triangle simplification error. A regular parent cluster can hold up to 128 bricks. Each brick has a 4 × 4 × 4 binary occupancy mask. [Epic group reduction](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/ClusterDAG.cpp#L1491) · [Source audit](NANITE_FOLIAGE_PIPELINE_REVIEW.md#how-the-bake-preserves-coverage-and-materials)

At 2160 pixels high and 45° vertical FOV, a transverse pixel spans `2 × view depth × tan(22.5°) / 2160`. At the 100 m target that is 3.84 cm, or **3.64–3.96 cm across the actual bough vertices** at the benchmark yaw. Start with world-space edges of **1.9, 2.9, 3.8 and 5.8 cm** (about 0.5, 0.75, 1 and 1.5 px at target depth). Include the observed native range if it falls outside this bracket. At 50–58 m a target-depth pixel spans 1.92–2.22 cm. These values set the search range. Qualification uses rendered images and per-group projected bounds.

1. Keep the current leaf-and-twig triangle cut as control. First replace leaves only, drawing exactly the control's twig triangles. Then try a joint leaf-and-twig cut at the best qualifying sizes, retaining material identity and thin exposed wood. Each candidate owns an explicit set of source placements and material slots. Selecting it replaces that set exactly once. Try an assembled group beside a reusable part if the native trace shows appreciable cross-spray merging or the part bake misses the crown gaps.
2. Compare our binary coverage fitter with an Epic-style bake at equal cell size and brick budget. The native fixture uses 64 rays, normal-distribution fitting and binary occupancy. Port the coverage redistribution that transfers sampled coverage from discarded cells to neighbors. Start with the existing normals and lighting on both custom arms to isolate the occupancy change. Use the existing tight brick boxes and first-hit depth shader. [Verified native settings](../build/unreal-nanite-oak/verified-assemblies.json) · [Coverage fitter](../Tools/VoxelCoverageOptimizer.cpp) · [Tight brick results](NANITE_FOLIAGE_PIPELINE_REVIEW.md#tight-brick-proxy-results)
3. Keep the bake, selector and renderer separable. The bake emits material-specific masks and attributes, source membership, bounds, measured error and work counts. Force candidates for the first comparison. Once measured, let each group choose the cheapest qualifying triangle or voxel candidate using projected error. Initially use the same triangle shadow casters for all camera cuts, then evaluate shadow selection independently.

## 3. Qualify the result and decide

- **Coverage and motion.** Run isolated and whole-crown 4K masks at the benchmark yaw. Reuse the 11 bough angles and reserve fresh orbit angles before fitting. Check occupied-pixel IoU and the fraction of outline pixels missing by over one pixel against each renderer's finest reference, using the accepted triangle quality gates wherever available. Also report native Voxelize quality, separate leaf/twig coverage, gaps, and p95 first-hit depth on shared pixels. Inspect shaded leaf brightness and twig visibility. Run a continuous retreat and return through the mixed interval, first with frozen wind and then with wind. Compare successive frames against a matching triangle-reference sequence and check ground shadows at low sun. [Existing bough gates](OAK_REUSABLE_SPRAYS_41_100.md#what-was-compared)
- **Cost.** Alternate at least four warmed 61-frame runs on the RTX 5090 with identical cameras, material settings, frustum culling and HZB off. Report paired camera, shadow and frame GPU times and run-to-run spread. Record source triangles retained, submitted box triangles, occupied cells, drawn bricks, visible voxel pixels, bake size and resident bytes. Our indexed boxes submit 12 proxy triangles per brick. Replacing the 39,663 leaf triangles on this pose leaves room for **3,305 bricks** before the submitted triangle count rises. Use GPU time to decide whether the trade pays. Report native UE visibility plus base-pass timing separately. [Renderer counters](../src/BranchVoxelBenchmark.cpp) · [Per-material baseline](../build/oak-assembly-step/reusable/depth-materials.csv)
- **Decision.** Advance to the interior bough if the exposed candidate passes the image gates, beats the freshly timed triangle control beyond run-to-run variation at 100 m, and retains the saving when inserted into the crown. Beating the same-camera Nanite triangle replay is a second milestone. If masks fail, use the per-material and gap measurements to revise the bake or keep triangles in the failing groups. If quality passes but GPU time loses, measure brick submissions and projected box overdraw before investing in a different rasterizer. Keep the 24–41 m whole-crown triangle work on its own track.
