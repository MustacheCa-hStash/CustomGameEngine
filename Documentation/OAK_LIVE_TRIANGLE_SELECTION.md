## What runs in the viewer

The Unreal editor exports the resident hierarchy layout and captures its physical cluster pages. The packer reads native hierarchy child bounds, parent and child errors, assembly transforms, cluster errors, geometry and material ranges. The viewer transforms and projects those bounds for its current camera, traverses the hierarchy, and draws the selected clusters. No camera angle, distance or selected-cluster list is baked into a sidecar. The native-selection check reproduced all six primary Unreal triangle counts. The D3D masks agreed with the native masks at 99.9546–100% occupied-pixel IoU across the initial 58 comparisons. Both renderers selected triangles throughout this experiment. [UE export](../Tools/CaptureOakSelector.py) · [Packer](../Tools/PackOakLiveCuts.py) · [Live traversal and submission](../src/D3D12Renderer.cpp) · [Per-view checks](../build/oak-live-selection/verification.csv)

The renderer can submit one draw per selected cluster placement, instance all placements using identical cluster geometry, or additionally gather the selected assembly-space indices into one draw per material. The reusable alternative tests the projected bounds of **one spray** and selects a baked leaf-and-twig tier at that scale. Its draws group placements by selected geometry. The material-mixed candidate selects leaves from the native hierarchy, twigs from the reusable tiers, and the trunk from our existing hierarchy. All variants share the same material submission interface. The unfolded native whole-crown diagnostic package occupies 159,939,092 bytes and the reusable whole-crown package occupies 907,884 bytes. These sizes include decoded D3D draw data. ## Why the twig count diverged

At 100 m and 132°, the previous grouped picker spends 34,384 actual twig triangles on the exposed bough. The live UE cut spends 24,662. That accounts for 9,722 of the 15,224 extra triangles (63.9%). Our per-spray decision spends 20,272 on twigs and 35,631 on leaves, totaling 55,903. It passes both primary silhouette gates. Each isolated material counter includes two degenerate fixture triangles, removed here. UE's builder merges and reduces cluster groups and propagates geometric errors. At runtime a cluster can cross its projected threshold independently of the rest of a spray or the other sprays in a 12-placement group. UE has no need for a material-specific rule to discard twigs. We also observed the reverse at 41 m, where native bough 7 retained 69,078 twig triangles and our 1 px cut retained 49,277. ## Quality and timing protocol

Each static candidate must meet or exceed native 1 px occupied-pixel IoU and match or improve its fraction of outline pixels farther than one pixel from a matching edge. Edges include gaps and are counted in both directions. We score against both Unreal's finest triangle silhouette and the D3D silhouette of the same input. The latter uses fixed LOD 4 for the full crown. The artist-source mesh is substantially finer than the geometry imported into UE. Cross-rasterizer differences near the cutoff can make the exact native cut narrowly miss a strict D3D gate. The data include the native 1 px reference as well as separate settings calibrated to clear both gates on the primary view. [Calibration](../build/oak-live-selection/calibration.csv) · [Budget refinement](../build/oak-live-selection/refinement.csv)

Shaded timings use alternating order and four independent 61-frame runs per arm on an RTX 5090. The summary reports the median of run medians. GPU camera includes sky and ground. CPU frame work runs through command-list submission and excludes the fence wait. CPU selection measures hierarchy tests and binning. CPU frame work also includes transform or index packing and draw recording. The historical native Unreal figures measure Nanite visibility plus base pass on the same assets and cameras. The D3D native-selection column draws the actual Nanite-selected geometry with our shading and measures the same passes as other D3D arms. ## Submission and vertex cost

At 100 m, one draw per native placement costs 13,015 shaded camera draws on the crown. Instancing identical geometry gives 5,365. Packing assembly-space indices gives 10, with the same full-color output at the tested camera. The last change takes the shaded camera from 0.249 to 0.240 ms and CPU work from 0.605 to 0.722 ms. At 41 m it takes 5,089 draws to 17 and GPU time from 0.483 to 0.476 ms. CPU work rises from 0.675 to 0.823 ms. The gather copies selected indices into an upload buffer every frame. The hierarchy and submission policy can be selected independently. [Shaded pixel checks](../build/oak-live-selection/shaded-agreement.csv) · [Individual timing runs](../build/oak-live-selection/timing.csv)

At the original 1 px setting, the 100 m reusable crown with our trunk draws 986,153 triangles and invokes the vertex shader 1.994M times. The native cut draws 1,073,068 triangles and invokes it 1.523M times with ordinary instancing. Pixel shader invocations differ by about 1%. Rendering each material separately identifies the trunk as the largest contributor to the vertex gap at 100 m. Our trunk draws 288,355 triangles and costs 716,970 vertex invocations. The native trunk draws 400,563 and costs 411,639. The leaf difference is 148,129 invocations. At 41 m, leaves account for the largest difference, 2.167M versus 1.451M vertex invocations. These are direct hardware counters. Single-material runs retain the same ground and shadow pass. [Hardware counters](../build/oak-live-selection/pipeline-counters.csv) · [Material isolation](../build/oak-live-selection/material-counters.csv)

An offline reorder into 64-vertex meshlets preserved every oriented triangle and vertex attribute in both static trunk cuts. Shaded camera time increased from 0.514 to 0.524 ms at 41 m and 0.286 to 0.293 ms at 100 m. The 100 m vertex count rose by 85,750 invocations, and 537 shaded pixels differed by more than one display code. The original trunk order remains the control. ## Crown coverage and motion

The original per-spray cut combined with the existing trunk passed 7/13 crown angles at 41 m and 13/13 at 100 m. Substituting the native trunk, with the very same per-spray choices, passed all 13 angles at each distance. This combination uses 2,660,326 and 1,098,361 triangles at 132°. Relative to the full native triangle selections, that is +5.8% and +2.4%. Replacing only the finer spray tiers with the conservative setting costs another 789,518 triangles at the 41 m primary view. Both arrangements met the static crown gates. Camera-level qualification and isolated-bough qualification answer different visibility questions. [Full-crown mask results](../build/oak-live-selection/canopy-quality.csv) · [Original crown sweep](../build/oak-live-selection/crown-screen.csv)

The moving test runs 31 frames from 41 to 100 m while orbiting 31°, then reverses the camera path. We repeated both legs with frozen and animated wind. Each cut is compared with the same-frame D3D finest geometry and wind pose. Temporal error is the disagreement between the candidate and source frame-to-frame mask changes, divided by their four-mask union. With wind, the reusable canopy and native trunk reached 96.54% worst-frame IoU and 5.76% p95 temporal error, versus 96.02% / 5.98% for the complete native cut in D3D and 96.01% / 6.18% for the previous pilot. Frozen-wind values were 96.60% / 5.66%, 96.16% / 6.01% and 96.07% / 6.22%. The stock grid budget stays fixed through each motion test. Performance samples use the two fixed endpoint cameras. ## Build and review

Run from the engine repository root. The command builds Release, stages the existing matched benchmark inputs and opens the interactive camera on the fastest complete-frame candidate. Use distance 100 for the second pose. Use arm `reusable-native-trunk` for the closest reusable triangle count, `native-compact` for the complete native hierarchy in D3D, or `current` for the previous two-bough pilot. The independent shipping-grid fixture is `production`. Subjects `7` and `8` isolate the boughs. Use `-Arm sprays -PixelError 1` to inspect the 55,903-triangle exposed cut at 100 m. [Build and review script](../Tools/BuildOakLiveCuts.ps1)

```powershell
.\Tools\BuildOakLiveCuts.ps1 -View -Arm tiered -Distance 41
```

`-Repack` builds camera-independent packages from the resident readbacks. `-Recapture` builds the editor module and regenerates the native hierarchy readbacks and fresh reference views from the staged UE 5.8 project. `-Benchmark` runs the mask, shadow-counter, timing, GPU invocation and motion comparisons. These options use the existing optimized input, UE project and regional bake fixtures from the preceding oak experiments. [Input and bake preparation](OAK_REUSABLE_SPRAYS_41_100.md#build-and-view)
