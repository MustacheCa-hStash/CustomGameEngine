# White oak triangle budget and Nanite comparison

**Start with the leaf bake at 24–41 m and the twig parents at 100 m.** On the shared 4K oak, our cheapest sampled cuts that match Nanite on two image measures use 4% more triangles at 14 m, 2% more at 18 m, 22% more at 24 m, 74% more at 41 m, and 135% more at 100 m. The near differences approach the uncertainty of the full-input control. The two larger gaps have different causes and need different geometry.

## What the cameras actually draw

I remeasured the archived offscreen masks from UE 5.8.4 and the custom renderer on the forked oak. Both use the same trunk, 4,730 transforms, 1,504 leaf and 950 twig triangles per input spray, a 3840 × 2160 target, 45° vertical FOV, frozen wind, frustum culling, and triangle-only rendering with depth occlusion off. The counters measure tree triangles queued for rasterization. Each renderer is judged against its own finest reference. The two fixed masks overlap by 99.66–99.70%. The custom source contains 12,412,997 triangles before culling. The finest Unreal controls at 14 and 100 m count 1.3–2.1% fewer. [Capture setup and calibration](OAK_MATCHED_TRIANGLE_COMPARISON.md)

¹ Cheapest **sampled** custom cut whose occupied-mask intersection-over-union (IoU) equals or exceeds Nanite’s and whose fraction of unmatched edge pixels is at most Nanite’s. An edge is a tree pixel next to an in-frame empty pixel across a horizontal or vertical edge, including gaps inside the canopy. An edge matches if the other mask has an edge within one Euclidean pixel center-to-center. Both directions count, divided by the sum of the two edge counts. This measures visible outline placement at native resolution. Aligned outlines can still conceal a changed leaf, material, depth, or shadow. The settings in the table are diagnostic per-pose samples. At 14 m our nominal cut has 0.458% of edge samples beyond one pixel versus Nanite’s 0.003%. At 41 m, matching IoU alone gives 3.835M triangles with 97.026% overlap against Nanite’s 97.021%. The edge misses are 5.09% versus 4.86%. Adding the edge condition selects 4.374M, with 4.75% misses. A finer sweep between 0.95 and 1.00 px could lower this sampled cost. At 100 m the 2.523M cut matches IoU to 0.002 percentage points and has fewer edge misses (5.97% versus 6.70%). The far gap survives either comparison. Masks at the five nominal poses also match the subsequent batched two-pass replay pixel for pixel, with occlusion both on and off. ## What to borrow from Nanite

| Capability | Current implementation and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Projected selection and instancing | Both engines reuse assembly parts, choose detail from projected error and can use a different cut for the light. Our baker measures sampled surface distances in both directions and checks open leaf rims. Our batch shader projects a conservative wind-expanded bound. Its fallback spray plans choose local cuts using one scale for the entire spray part. A nearby tip or a difficult leaf can hold finer geometry elsewhere in that plan. Automatic forked leaves also bottom out in detail at **LOD 3**, whose measured source rim deviation is nonzero. A close-up can therefore exceed the nominal source budget. Document the authored near reference or provide a measured finer fallback where it matters. [Custom cut plans](../src/D3D12Renderer.cpp) · [Projection and plan selection](../shaders/TreeBatch.hlsl) · [Leaf bake report](../build/Release/bakes/WhiteOak-ForkedCanopy.leaves.fixedlod.txt) · [Nanite projected tests](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Shaders/Private/Nanite/NaniteClusterCulling.usf) |
| Geometry hierarchy | In UE’s local source, triangle clusters target up to 128 triangles. The builder groups clusters using shared edges and locality, simplifies merged children, then splits parents into clusters again. It locks external edge positions during simplification. Parents of a replacement share a bound and an error at least as large as their children’s. Our meshlets also cap triangle count, but scan existing index order for drawing and culling. The canopy hierarchy spatially bisects placements and bakes geometry only for two size bands, about 74 and 296 sprays here. Each eligible leaf or wood node has one proxy. Many potentially useful intermediate replacements have no representation. [Nanite reduction](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/ClusterDAG.cpp) · [Nanite simplification](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/Cluster.cpp) · [Custom meshlets](../src/MeshletPacking.h) · [Canopy and trunk bake](../Tools/BakeSprayBough.cpp) |
| Foliage simplification | Both systems preserve foliage area. Nanite can redistribute lost area over surviving open edges of the affected material during group reduction. Our fitted leaf keeps its longitudinal extent and area, with explicit rim and UV controls. On bough 7 the compensated and uncompensated cuts used the same triangle count. Compensation reduced the front-view coverage bias from −0.85% to 0.00% at reporting precision. This is a demonstrated benefit of the oak-specific fit. The current parent recipe still grants every leaf its own triangle or quad. [Epic’s area compensation](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Source/Developer/MeshSimplifier/Private/MeshSimplify.cpp) · [Bough controls](OAK_BOUGH_4K_RESULTS.md) |
| Visibility and throughput | We already have batched GPU cut selection, indirect drawing and two-pass camera HZB with recovery. At 64 trees and 41 m, HZB reduced camera time from 14.95 to 11.47 ms. At one matched tree it cost time at every measured distance. The optional meshlet frustum split at 64 trees and 41 m saved 4.2% of triangles but expanded 250 packets to 11,146 and cost 3.19 ms. Geometry simplification saves work on fully visible crowns too. Nanite also has a dedicated small-triangle raster path and compressed, streamable cluster pages. Our tree hierarchy sidecar alone is 148.4 MiB, shared across copies of this oak. [Two-pass replay](OAK_TWO_PASS_OCCLUSION_4K.md) · [Cluster-frustum replay](OAK_CLUSTER_FRUSTUM_4K.md) · [Epic’s architecture overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/nanite-virtualized-geometry-in-unreal-engine) |
| Far representation | Nanite’s builder tests a voxel candidate against triangle simplification error and cell/brick budgets per group. The forked-tree camera can mix representations in UE. Our regional voxel path has been exercised on the isolated branch. The older 41 m native-viewport optimized oak used 88,480 triangles **plus 202,656 bricks** with voxels enabled. It had a different projection path from the matched offscreen runs. Use that as evidence to try a representation, and charge the brick work when comparing it. ## Ranked experiments

1. **Replace the mid-distance leaf bottleneck on one reusable spray.** At the two-measure cuts there are 5.104M leaf triangles at 24 m and 2.671M at 41 m. Neither pose accepts a leaf parent. The baker already tries intermediate per-leaf budgets, locks two rim anchors, and rejects collapsed UVs. First replay identical candidates using each local group’s own projected bound to price the effect of the shared spray bound. Then compare those results with fitted intermediate leaf outlines and small patches that can redistribute area among overlapping leaves. Measure the completed patch directly against both references so accumulated worst-case leaf errors do not veto it unnecessarily. Keep exposed tips and cross-lit openings in the validation views. A change on one shared spray reaches 4,730 placements. [Local candidate generator](../Tools/BakeFixedLod.cpp) · [24 m counters](../build/oak-gpu-selection/matched/coverage/q24-0.875.timings.csv) · [41 m counters](../build/oak-gpu-selection/matched/coverage/q41-0.95.timings.csv)
2. **Replace the twig repair strategy on one small and one large parent.** At the 100 m comparable cut, wood parents cover every spray and draw 1,450,965 twig triangles. Of these, **683,447 (47.1%)** were appended by the repair loop after grid reduction. The larger parents likewise restored 413,670 of 863,617 triangles. The loop measures each reference face against the initial retained proxy and appends the entire failed face. Test a local, attribute-aware refit of connected failed regions, with joint and exposed-tip constraints. A second candidate can fit tapered segments where the source supports them. Re-evaluate the final surface, depth, and normals in both directions. Even recovering all 683k would cover less than half the 1.449M far gap. The 783k leaves plus 288k trunk already consume almost the entire Nanite total before drawing any twigs. [Repair loop and proxy bake](../Tools/BakeSprayBough.cpp) · [Per-parent restore log](../build/oak-gpu-selection/assets.log) · [100 m part counts](../build/oak-gpu-selection/matched/coverage/q100-0.489.timings.csv)
3. **Let several complete parent cuts compete for the same region.** Begin on one bough. Compare the current two scales against smaller spatial or branch-connected groups, multiple intermediate triangle budgets, and a joint leaf/wood candidate. Preserve material ranges and wind ownership when replacing a region. A joint candidate can take advantage of foliage hiding a twig from several angles while the light view still picks detailed wood. Add a far trunk/limb candidate once the combined canopy measurements show what trunk budget remains. Keep only replacements that improve measured cost at an accepted error, and retain fine children for every rejected parent. This borrows Nanite’s repeated merge/reduce principle while preserving the reusable spray and indexed draw path. [Current hierarchy record](../src/TreeHierarchy.h) · [Nanite assembly construction](../../UnrealEngine/Engine/Source/Developer/NaniteBuilder/Private/NaniteAssemblyBuild.cpp)
4. **Time one far voxel bough against the best triangle bough.** Reuse the branch occupancy baker on a complete forked-tree region, with the rest of the crown present. Record triangle count, bricks, occupied cells, raster time, shadow cost and transition quality. Use the 16- and 64-tree stands to decide when HZB pays. Reserve rasterizer and streaming work for measured bottlenecks after these bake trials. The existing 64-tree 41 m batched test spent 14.63 ms drawing the camera cut and about 0.11 ms traversing, selecting and packing it. ## Modular implementation and acceptance

Keep **candidate generation** offline. The meshoptimizer reducer, leaf fitter, twig fitter and voxel baker can each propose a replacement for the same identified source region. Give each proposal the covered source IDs, reference mesh, bounds, material and wind ownership, representation, draw ranges and measured error. A shared evaluator can reject broken seams, missing children, excessive rim/surface error, or unstable coverage and keep the useful cost/error choices. Store source-relative and shared-input errors separately as the existing bake already does. Keep the runtime cut selector responsible for a complete cover of visible regions and a per-view error budget. The existing batcher can consume triangle ranges, with a brick backend added when it earns its cost. This leaves oak-specific knowledge in the bake while allowing another tree species to use the same selector.

Use the five fixed poses as regression anchors. Fit candidates on separate views. Validate on held-out yaw, side/backlight, under-canopy, moving approach/retreat and deterministic wind, with source-relative eye-level checks. Compare internal openings and outer edges, first visible depth, shaded leaf response, temporal switching and caster/receiver error. For speed parity, replay the same stands in UE and custom with matching viewport, render scale, AA, wind, materials and warmed residency. Measure camera-only and shadows separately, then compare complete GPU frames with equivalent shadow quality and cache state. Report median and slow-frame time, CPU recording, memory, packets and primitives. Existing 4K evidence supports triangle comparisons. Existing cross-engine GPU scopes and stand coverage leave overall speed parity open. [Matched setup](OAK_MATCHED_TRIANGLE_COMPARISON.md) · [Timing scope differences](NANITE_OAK_COMPARISON.md)
