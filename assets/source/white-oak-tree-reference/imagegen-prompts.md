# White Oak Tree Reference

Both sheets were made with the built-in ImageGen tool. The Unreal screenshot informed the design feedback. The bare sheet was the image input for the summer edit.

The views run left to right from front to three-quarter to side. The angles and cross-view geometry are approximate.

## Bare structure generation prompt

Create a landscape-format, high-resolution photorealistic tree artist turnaround reference sheet with THREE equal-width views across ONE row. There is exactly ONE tree design seen from three camera azimuths: 0°, 45°, and 90° from left to right. It is a leafless, healthy, open-grown, mature eastern white oak (Quercus alba). This is the structural master for a later paired leaf-on sheet and must show credible trunk-to-twig architecture. Show the whole tree including the root flare and every terminal twig, same scale, centered in each third with a little room around it, identical eye-level nearly orthographic camera height, same neutral light cool-gray seamless backdrop and ground, same ground contact line and soft diffuse studio daylight. No words, lines, split-pane borders, measurement marks, debris, snow, pedestals, leaves, or background objects. Landscape aspect 3:1 or as wide as supported, with a compact crop in the vertical direction around the set of equally sized trees. Optimize for readability, no tiny distant trees.
TREE DESIGN, maintain across the three views: A single sturdy continuous trunk rises from a restrained flared base with believable bark-to-ground contact. The first 25 percent of tree height is mostly exposed trunk and is upright with a small natural lean. The trunk leads upward visibly and tapers, dividing into the crown a little off center. One particularly low long lateral bough extends to the viewer's left at 0°, traveling broadly out across the lower crown, with small direction changes and a modest dip, ending in many fine sprays. A second, shorter bough reaches to the right from slightly higher. Another bough extends out of the first image plane and becomes prominent at 90°. Near the upper center, one meandering short offset leader dissolves into finer branches. Aim for about six main scaffold limbs at irregular heights and azimuths plus a natural central leader, and a deep, roughly round dome of twig tips a little wider than the total tree height, no geometric symmetry. Natural angular changes and paths with a variety of pitch, broadly lateral lower limbs, some angled upper growth. No repetitive upright sweeping hooks, co-dominant trunks at the base, inflated limbs, brushlike spikes, propagating equal-width forks, excessive dead wood, or horizontal tiers. Each scaffold limb tapers distinctly, and secondary and tertiary branches get much finer at each fork. Show enough smaller, asymmetric branchlets and finely ramified live twigs to convincingly carry a dense foliage canopy. Preserve pockets of open space between limb systems so the branching can be read. Bark light gray-brown, moderately ridged and scaly, softer finer transitions out to smooth narrow twigs, realistic collars without seams. Natural muted tree color, excellent line definition and fine material detail like a serious botanical / VFX tree look-development reference, thoroughly lifelike with no illustrative outlines. Rotate the same recognizable structure with realistic foreshortening: the lowest long bough is longest in projection in the first view, shorter in the center, and largely projects into depth at right. Every view should feel like it is looking at a different side of this same tree.

## Summer foliage edit prompt

Edit this exact wide three-view artist reference into a companion full summer leaf-on reference for the same open-grown mature eastern white oak, Quercus alba. Use the entire reference image as an edit, retaining the three trees' placement and height in frame, angle and scale in each panel, and cool neutral gray backdrop and ground. Keep each root flare, trunk and bark exactly as they are. Keep every major limb in the position shown. From left to right, the hero front, a three-quarter view, and a side view go with the bare render. Match the unusually low long lateral bough on the left in the first two views and its foreshortening in the third, the higher shorter opposite limb, the short offset leader, and subtle asymmetries present in each frame. Don't shift the trees, crop any crown, expand the outer silhouette beyond a small natural margin of leaf growth, add background detail, write captions, add dividers, or change the wide aspect ratio.
Only add living foliage carried by the existing fine twig architecture. Mature open-grown white oak in full leaf, a broad rounded, lightly irregular silhouette with heavy, full, visually connected masses of real leaves. Use botanically plausible, naturally scaled small, matte to mildly lustrous oak leaves with rounded lobes, true-to-life proportions, tonal and orientation variation, soft light transmission and shadows between leaves. Much of the canopy is dense and connected, including near the top, with slightly uneven scalloped edges shaped by branches and small uneven holes that reveal a little inner branch structure. The lowest lateral bough carries a full but slightly distinct low skirt of foliage with a few visible spaces above or beside, integrated naturally into the crown. Some foliage is in front of the boughs, some behind, creating genuine depth. Rich but restrained summer green, olive in some shadows and lighter green on softly lit tips, natural dark inner layers, clearly resolved leaf clusters. No unnaturally giant leaves, bare whips poking through the crown, fluffy cloud forms, isolated pom-poms, topiary, neon greens, or uniform green curtain. Keep the exposed lower trunk and the underside of a few branches readable. A realistic VFX botanical reference plate, clean and detailed. The key is that this is the original tree in each corresponding view with summer leaf growth.
